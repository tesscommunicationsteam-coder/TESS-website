🏅 2027 AMS Fellow

Congratulations to Dr. Sen Chiao

Director, NOAA Cooperative Science Center in Atmospheric Sciences and Meteorology (NCAS-M) · Professor, Howard University

The Taiwanese-American Earth Science Society (TESS) warmly congratulates Dr. Sen Chiao on being elected a 2027 Fellow of the American Meteorological Society (AMS), one of the Society's highest honors recognizing outstanding contributions to the atmospheric and related sciences.

Dr. Chiao's distinguished career has advanced atmospheric science through research in numerical weather prediction, atmospheric dynamics, air quality, and climate, while his leadership has strengthened research, education, and workforce development.

TESS is honored that Dr. Chiao has agreed to serve as a TESS Advisor and a speaker for the upcoming TESS Professional Development Roundtable. We sincerely congratulate him on this well-deserved recognition and wish him continued success in research, education, and scientific leadership.

Message from the President

Why TESS exists

President
Ying-Hwa (Bill) Kuo
Taiwanese-American Earth Science Society

Four decades of U.S.–Taiwan collaboration in earth science

For over forty years, U.S.–Taiwan collaboration in earth system science has advanced both nations and the broader scientific community. From the 1987 Taiwan Area Mesoscale Experiment (TAMEX) — the first major mesoscale field experiment in East Asia — to the 2006 COSMIC satellite mission that delivered the world's first real-time constellation of GNSS radio occultation data, these partnerships have shaped modern weather prediction and climate monitoring.

Taiwanese scientists working in the U.S. have played a unique and critical role, understanding and connecting the science communities on both sides of the Pacific. Yet many work in isolation, with little networking among fellow Taiwanese scientists across the field.

As first-generation immigrants, Taiwanese-American scientists face challenges including language barriers, cultural differences, and communication hurdles that can slow career progression. TESS was created to change that — providing mentorship, connection, and a professional home.

Event Highlight

Celebrating our launch

June 27, 2026 · Boulder, CO

Dragon Boat Festival · 2026 TESS 端午同歡

On a bright summer Saturday, the TESS community gathered in Boulder for a relaxed lunch to celebrate the Dragon Boat Festival (端午節) — one of the most cherished holidays in Taiwanese culture. Members, families, and friends came together over good food and warm conversation, strengthening the sense of community that TESS was founded to nurture.

The gathering offered a welcome chance for Taiwanese-American earth scientists across all career stages to unwind, reconnect, and welcome newcomers in a friendly, informal setting. Events like this remind us that TESS is not only a professional network, but also a home away from home.

Taiwan Earth System Model (TaiESM) — Science Seminar

March 18, 2026 · NSF NCAR Mesa Laboratory

TESS Launch Luncheon — celebrating our establishment

TESS hosted its first event celebrating its official launch and long-term U.S.–Taiwan collaboration in atmospheric sciences. Around 40 participants attended, including Director General Debby Huang (TECO Denver), Dr. Li-Wei Chou (TECO Los Angeles), and senior leaders from UCAR, NCAR, and NOAA.

Dr. Chou introduced funding programs for U.S.–Taiwan collaboration, while the TESS president presented the society's background, mission, and logo (designed by board member Chih-Ting Hsu).
